# Episode 2 — Give It a Job, Not Just a Prompt

**Video:** [Watch Episode 2](https://youtu.be/T0Lq0j8nEqU)

**Goal:** Turn a vague request into a small assignment with a result, audience, allowed information and tools, checks, and a stopping point. Decide which missing facts need a question and which should remain visibly unresolved.

**Audience:** Professionals and business owners who do not code.

## The five-part job brief

| Field | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Result | What should it produce? |
| Audience | Who needs the result? |
| Allowed information and tools | What may it use? |
| Checks | What makes the result useful? |
| Stopping point | Where does the job end? |

A clear assignment gives you something concrete to review. A longer prompt does not guarantee a better result or create an autonomous agent. Prompt instructions describe boundaries; actual workspace permissions control access.

## The exercise

Use ChatGPT in a browser, or another suitable AI workspace, to turn fictional meeting notes into a project brief. Compare a broad request with an explicit job, answer decision-critical questions, and check the resulting draft against the supplied facts. This exercise requires no browsing or app connections.

Use two fresh conversations with the same model and the same original notes where practical. The second run receives additional human answers. It demonstrates clarification and a clearer handoff, rather than a controlled test of prompt length. Keep useful work from either run.

### Fictional practice notes

Copy this entire block after each prompt below, before submitting it:

```text
FICTIONAL PRACTICE NOTES
Harbor Lane Consulting wants a landing page for a new workshop.
Audience: owners of small service businesses.
The discussed scope is one page with a form to register interest, not to take payments.
The form destination and website platform have not been chosen.
The client would like the page ready about two weeks after kickoff. No kickoff date has been agreed.
The working budget is about $3,000, subject to confirmation.
The client will supply copy and images. No delivery date for those materials is confirmed.
John will prepare the first project brief. Alex is the client's review contact. The final approver has not been confirmed.
Success means a visitor can understand the workshop and register interest. No numerical target has been agreed.
Payments, booking integrations, a wider website redesign, and ongoing maintenance were not included in the discussion.
We need a brief before the next planning conversation.
All names and project details here are fictional.
```

## Run A — start with a broad request

Paste this prompt and the notes into the first fresh conversation:

```text
Help me get this workshop landing-page project organized. Use only the fictional notes below and draft your response here. Do not browse, access connected apps, send messages, create external files, or take any project action.
```

Inspect what it actually returns. What output did it choose? What did it do with timing, budget, approval, and platform decisions? A useful checklist or a good question is a valid result. Do not manufacture a failure to justify a longer prompt.

## Run B — define a reviewable job

Paste this prompt and the same original notes into the second fresh conversation:

```text
Prepare a project brief for the client sponsor and the person who will build the workshop landing page.

Result: a plain-language brief of no more than 450 words. It should let those readers understand the proposed work, the important unknowns, and what needs a decision before work starts.

Use only the fictional practice notes below and answers I provide in this conversation. Do not invent dates, budgets, approvals, responsibilities, platforms, suppliers, or performance targets.

Include:
- Purpose and intended audience.
- Proposed deliverables and what is outside scope.
- Timing, budget, responsibilities, and dependencies, with their actual certainty.
- Open decisions and the next human decision needed.

Quality checks:
- Separate confirmed details from targets, proposals, assumptions, and unknowns. A proposal is not an agreed commitment.
- Trace important details to the notes or my answers.
- Keep dependencies visible, especially the client's copy and images.
- Do not select a platform or form provider.
- Keep the result usable for a reader who was not in the meeting.

Before drafting, ask up to three questions whose answers would most change scope, timing, cost, or approval. If there are more unknowns, preserve them in the brief rather than filling them in. Stop and wait for my answers. If no questions are necessary, explain why and wait for my instruction to draft.

After I answer, prepare the brief and a short separate list of unresolved decisions. Stop at the draft for my review.

Work only in this conversation. Do not browse, access connected apps, create external files, contact anyone, book anything, build the page, or spend money. These instructions describe the task; use a workspace whose actual permissions fit it.
```

Check whether it pauses as requested. Evaluate the questions it actually asks. If it drafts immediately, point out the missed pause and ask it to stop for clarification. If no questions are needed, inspect its explanation instead of forcing a question.

### Supply human answers

In the recorded demo, John confirms the proposed scope and approximate budget, leaves timing undecided, and names Alex as final approver. The rehearsal script contains a different, dated answer packet; those dates were not supplied in the recorded run.

For practice aligned with the video, reply in conversation B with:

```text
For this fictional exercise:
- The one-page interest-registration scope and approximate $3,000 budget are confirmed. Do not imply that a quote or contract has been accepted.
- Timing is not decided. Do not invent a kickoff date or turn the original two-week preference into an agreed deadline.
- Alex can give final approval.
- Client copy and images are still outstanding; their delivery date is unknown.
- The platform and form destination remain undecided. Do not choose them.

If your questions are not answered by these facts or the original notes, keep those questions unresolved. Now prepare the brief and the separate unresolved-decisions list according to the job above.
```

These are new facts for Run B. Do not present them as information available to Run A or as actual client commitments.

## Inspect the brief

Check meaning before polish. Trace at least one important statement to an original note or a human answer.

| Check | What to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | One page and an interest form; no payments, booking integration, redesign, or maintenance silently added. |
| Timing | No invented kickoff date or firm delivery commitment; undecided timing stays visible. |
| Budget | Approximate $3,000 budget; no invented quote acceptance, contract, or precise ceiling. |
| Approval | Alex is final approver because the human supplied that answer. |
| Dependencies | Outstanding copy and images and their unknown delivery date remain visible. |
| Open decisions | Platform and form destination remain undecided. |
| Output | A plain-language brief within 450 words, with unresolved decisions listed separately. |
| Stop | Draft returned in the conversation; no page built or external action taken. |

### Repair only a real problem

If the draft meets the job, keep it. If a requirement fails, use the actual sentence or omission:

```text
The brief does not meet this requirement: [paste the exact requirement].
The relevant sentence is: [paste the actual sentence, or say what is missing].
The source information is: [paste the supporting note or my answer].

Make the smallest correction needed. Preserve supported details and keep unresolved decisions visible. Return the corrected brief within the original 450-word limit. Then say exactly what you changed. Stop at the draft.
```

Compare the actual revision, not just its summary of changes. If the problem remains, mark the brief unfinished and make the necessary human edit.

The first result may be equally useful. A polished draft is not a project commitment, and drafting plus clarification does not establish that autonomous tool use occurred.

## Your small-task practice

Choose one task you can inspect in a few minutes. Use fictional or nonconfidential information. Fill in:

1. **Result I want:**
2. **Audience:**
3. **Allowed information and tools:**
4. **Checks before I call it useful:**
5. **Stopping point:**

Ask about missing facts that would change the work, preserve other unknowns, and run it once. Check an important statement against your inputs. Request a specific repair only if needed. Stop with a draft for your review.

## Checklist

- [ ] I defined all five parts of the job.
- [ ] I used fictional or nonconfidential inputs and appropriate workspace permissions.
- [ ] I inspected the actual clarification questions and output.
- [ ] I distinguished original notes from new human answers.
- [ ] I checked scope, timing, budget, approval, dependencies, and open decisions.
- [ ] I compared actual wording and corrected only a real problem.
- [ ] I stopped at a draft and kept commitments and external actions with the human.
