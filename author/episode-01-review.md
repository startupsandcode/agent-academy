## Before recording

- Confirm whether a recording exists.
- If not recorded, choose the public-search/browser demo tool, set the exact seven-day UTC window (seven complete UTC calendar days ending the day before recording), and rehearse the complete run.
- If recorded, review the footage and note any missing takes.
- Keep a clean recording window and hide private tabs/notifications. Use only public research access. Prepare genuine backup source records in case live browsing is unavailable.
- Do not add API keys, code, private inboxes, client data, sign-ins, or external actions to this lesson.

## Teaching and demo checks

- The full episode target is about 20 minutes (18–22 minutes); the genuine screen demonstration is 10–15 minutes. The reference demo is 13 minutes 20 seconds. Spoken teaching overlaps live actions.
- Keep the work visible: a direct request, an explicit research workflow, an actual source inspection, a truthful keep/discard decision, a correction only if one was actually found, and a comparison of actual before/after text.
- A source's article date can differ from its announcement/event date. Check the seven-day window and actual rollout, availability, plan/geography/eligibility limits.
- Attribute vendor claims; company statements do not independently prove savings, performance, market impact, or business suitability.
- Do not weaken the first run to guarantee a win for the more detailed request. The first answer may be equally good or better.
- A plan or “verifying sources” message alone is not evidence that research happened. If tools are unavailable, label the source-excerpt fallback human-assisted.
- Show the evidence log separately from the under-350-word final briefing. Fewer than three qualifying items is acceptable.
- Keep boundaries explicit: public sources only; no sign-in, forms, downloads, private data, spending, contact, sending, publishing, or recommendations acted on.
