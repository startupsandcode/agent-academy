## Teaching and continuity checks

- The demonstrated job is drafting a project brief from fictional Harbor Lane Consulting notes. It is not building or publishing a landing page.
- Five fields define the job: result, audience, allowed information and tools, checks, and stopping point.
- Run A can produce useful work. Do not imply that a broad request must fail or that a longer prompt guarantees improvement.
- Run B receives extra human answers. Keep that information difference explicit when comparing drafts.
- In the recording, John confirms the scope and approximate budget, leaves timing undecided, and says Alex can give final approval. The dated November 2 / November 16 answer packet belongs to the rehearsal script, not the recorded run. The workbook practice follows the recording's answers.
- Preserve outstanding copy and images, undecided platform/form destination, and uncertainty around timing. Do not imply a signed contract or accepted quote.
- Prompt boundaries do not configure actual access permissions. This demo uses supplied notes and clarification; it does not establish autonomous tool use.
- Repair a specific failure only when the returned draft actually has one. Keep a useful first draft.
- The next episode is about the right context and tools. Avoid an inconsistent promise that Episode 3 is primarily about execution.
